# Editorial depth audit

Checked: 2026-07-27

This audit tests whether a chapter is too compressed for a first-time reader. Word count is a signal, not the decision rule. A chapter is adequate when it states its scope, separates source tiers, explains its central mechanism, gives a usable example or procedure where appropriate, and names the main failure mode or evidential limit.

## Beginner-flow gates used in this pass

1. A technical term must receive a plain-language gloss in the same paragraph or immediately adjacent block. A rear glossary may reinforce the definition but cannot rescue an opaque first use.
2. Each chapter opening must say what prior idea it carries forward and what problem the new chapter solves.
3. A dense list needs an orientation before it and a synthesis or next action after it.
4. A repeated warning stays only when it adds a new threshold, decision rule, or source-boundary correction.
5. Canonical, commentarial, Mahāsi, health and research evidence, and editorial explanations must remain visibly distinct even when prose is simplified.
6. When one real-time decision depends on rules distributed across several chapters, the book needs a single retrieval aid that points back to the full explanations without silently rewriting them.

## Result

The original depth defects, the missing feeling-to-craving bridge and the compressed insight map, are corrected. The 2026-07-27 pass then removed unexplained first-use terms and abrupt conceptual handoffs; it added glossary rescue entries for the Four Noble Truths, Noble Eightfold Path, Five Precepts, contact, feeling-to-craving vocabulary, and grasping. The later structural pass split the overloaded attainment material into a focused treatment of the first three fetters and a separate 3–5–4 classification chapter, then moved the insight map to Chapter 12. Reader feedback still exposed two real failures: structural presence did not guarantee conceptual clarity, and a four-region summary still did not explain how a region becomes experientially intelligible. The attainment revision answered the first failure with a consistent three-fetter model and a plain four-fruit map. The first Chapter 12 revision added maturation criteria, practice conditions, phenomenology, transitions and counterevidence. A later hostile reread found that those answers still arrived too late and that stages 5–10 remained compressed relative to the rest. The next opening supplied the mental model before taxonomy; the rewritten middle gave every pre-equanimity stage the same six questions and kept the hard boundary against invented attainment recipes. A further beginner pass found that the information was present but still too easy to scan as dense prose. Chapter 12 exposed all seventeen names as navigable subheadings, distinguished voluntary conditions from insight that cannot be commanded, held one pain object stable across stages 1–11, expanded the late-system sequence without creating fake sensations, and ended with closed-book retrieval. The latest hostile pass found one remaining implicit assumption: the reader could still treat “mature,” “dominant” and “attained” as if the sources supplied an unstated numerical cutoff. The revised opening now separates three axes of change, states that no validated count, duration or concentration threshold exists, supplies five kinds of longitudinal and counterevidence, and works one “dissolution plus fear” report from raw words to three bounded outcomes. This added clarity does not turn the map into a classifier; it makes the absence of such a classifier explicit. The later practice pass found a third kind of failure: a correct technical noun such as “salient” can remain unusable until it drives a decision. Chapter 5 now turns its terms into an observable stay–switch–act loop and makes a consequential source-versus-safety conflict visible. The daily-life pass found the same defect in a different form: four anchors could prompt awareness but did not tell a reader how to allocate attention, act under time pressure, or recover after missing the pause. Chapter 7 now supplies those decisions without turning its editorial loops into scripture. The action-path pass separately lets the novice reach a safe first sit, the label lookup, the feeling-to-craving drill, an immediate grounding action, and one canonical restart route without assembling them from distant sections. A final retrieval audit then found that the live choices themselves remained distributed across four chapters. Appendix E now renders their relationship as one source-labelled, safety-first decision map in both print and semantic HTML, while sending the reader back to the full chapter whenever compression could hide a condition. The Vietnam-first safety pass closes another retrieval defect: a reader in immediate danger no longer has to leave the book to discover which official number to call, while the source and country boundaries remain visible. The whole-book learning pass found one remaining asymmetry: Chapters 3, 6, 8 and 9 explained their central distinctions but did not require the reader to reconstruct or act on them before moving on. Their new closing cards complete the chapter-level retrieval path without adding doctrinal claims, stage scores or vocabulary quizzes. Every chapter now passes the internal gates above, but that is an editorial finding, not novice-validation evidence.

This is not evidence that true beginners understand the book unaided. That claim still requires the external protocol in `beginner-validation-protocol.md`; a failure there overrides this internal verdict.
