# Editorial depth audit

Checked: 2026-07-27

This audit tests whether a chapter is too compressed for a first-time reader. Word count is a signal, not the decision rule. A chapter is adequate when it states its scope, separates source tiers, explains its central mechanism, gives a usable example or procedure where appropriate, and names the main failure mode or evidential limit.

## Beginner-flow gates used in this pass

1. A technical term must receive a plain-language gloss in the same paragraph or immediately adjacent block. A rear glossary may reinforce the definition but cannot rescue an opaque first use.
2. Each chapter opening must say what prior idea it carries forward and what problem the new chapter solves.
3. A dense list needs an orientation before it and a synthesis or next action after it.
4. A repeated warning stays only when it adds a new threshold, decision rule, or source-boundary correction.
5. Canonical, commentarial, Mahāsi, health and research evidence, and editorial explanations must remain visibly distinct even when prose is simplified.

## Result

The original depth defects, the missing feeling-to-craving bridge and the compressed insight map, are corrected. The 2026-07-27 pass then removed unexplained first-use terms, abrupt conceptual handoffs, and the compressed treatment of fetters and fruits; it also added glossary rescue entries for the Four Noble Truths, Noble Eightfold Path, Five Precepts, contact, feeling-to-craving vocabulary, and grasping. Chapter 10 now precedes the insight map so the book defines its titled destination before presenting a later reference system. Every chapter now passes the internal gates above.

This is not evidence that true beginners understand the book unaided. That claim still requires the external protocol in `beginner-validation-protocol.md`; a failure there overrides this internal verdict.
